2. Is the Stock Market for Trading or Investing?
The stock market offers opportunities for both investing and trading, depending on your financial goals, risk tolerance, and time horizon.

Investing is focused on long-term wealth creation. Investors buy fundamentally strong companies and hold them for years to benefit from business growth, compounding, and capital appreciation.

Trading, on the other hand, focuses on capturing short-term price movements. Skilled traders use technical analysis, price action, and disciplined risk management to identify high-probability opportunities. While trading has the potential to generate higher returns in a shorter period, it also involves higher risk and requires continuous learning, discipline, and a well-defined trading strategy.

5. What are the most important chart types in the stock market?
The most commonly used chart types for market analysis include:
• Line Chart – Best for understanding overall price trends.
• Bar Chart – Displays the opening, high, low, and closing prices for each trading session.
• Candlestick Chart – The most widely used chart by traders, offering detailed insights into market sentiment and price action through single and multiple candlestick patterns.
6. What common mistakes should beginners avoid in the stock market?
Many beginners make avoidable mistakes that can impact their trading journey. Some of the most common include:
1. Chasing quick profits.
2. Investing without proper research.
3. Blindly following social media tips or market rumours.
4. Overtrading by buying and selling too frequently.

7. What is the difference between trading and investing?
Trading focuses on short-term price movements, where traders aim to generate profits over days, weeks, or months through frequent transactions.

Investing, on the other hand, is a long-term approach focused on building wealth by holding fundamentally strong stocks for several years.
8. What is Elliott Wave Theory?
Elliott Wave Theory is a technical analysis method developed to identify recurring market cycles and predict future price movements. Based on the observation that financial markets move in repetitive wave patterns driven by investor psychology, the theory helps traders understand where the market is likely to move next.

The modern application of Elliott Wave Theory combines wave analysis with market structure, price action, and risk management to improve trading decisions. By identifying impulse waves and corrective waves, traders can anticipate potential trend reversals, continuation patterns, and high-probability trading opportunities.
9. What are Fibonacci Retracement and Fibonacci Extension?
Fibonacci Retracement and Fibonacci Extension are powerful technical analysis tools used to identify potential support, resistance, entry, exit, and profit target levels. They are widely used alongside Elliott Wave Theory and Price Action analysis.
10. Are chart patterns like Flag, Head and Shoulders, Inverse Head and Shoulders, and Cup and Handle outdated?
Yes. Traditional chart patterns such as Bull Flag, Bear Flag, Head and Shoulders, Inverse Head and Shoulders, and Cup and Handle are still widely used by traders around the world. However, today's financial markets have become much more dynamic due to increased institutional participation, algorithmic trading, and faster information flow.

At Checkmate Investrado Academy, we believe that while these classic patterns remain valuable, traders can gain a deeper understanding of market structure by learning advanced analytical techniques such as Elliott Wave Theory, Wolfe Wave, Price Action, and Fibonacci Analysis.
